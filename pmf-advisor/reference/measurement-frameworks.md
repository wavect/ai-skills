# Interpreting surveys and cohort retention

Set survey eligibility before sending invitations. Use people who have experienced the core benefit within the product's natural usage cycle. Describe the selection bias this creates. Record eligible, invited, answered and very disappointed counts. Ask which benefit respondents would miss and examine whether the same segment returns or renews. The 40% benchmark came from comparisons across startups. Its applicability to this product is a research question. [Superhuman founder's account](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)

For retention, fix the unit (person or account), starting event, return event and elapsed interval. Compare only cohorts eligible for that interval. If 12 of 30 January accounts and 4 of 10 February accounts reached a second monthly close, both cohorts have 40% retention at that point. A March cohort aged two weeks has no month-two observation. Show counts next to rates and investigate changes in acquisition mix.

For roadmap sorting, RICE = reach × impact × confidence / effort. Compare candidates within one time horizon. Write the predicted link from feature to value event and test the weakest link before treating a numerical lead as decisive.

For a scale decision, connect repeat use to acquisition economics. Ask how many paid cycles at the observed contribution margin it takes to recover the cost of acquiring one account. Look for that many cycles of follow-up before treating a favorable first month as evidence for a larger spend.
