# Willingness-to-pay research

Keep the segment, package, currency and billing interval fixed across all four Van Westendorp questions. Collect each respondent's thresholds for too cheap to trust, a bargain, expensive but still worth considering, and too expensive. Check ordering and missing answers. Disclose exclusions.

At each candidate price, count the share still calling it too cheap or cheap (decreasing curves), and the share already calling it expensive or too expensive (increasing curves). The optimal price point is the intersection of too cheap and too expensive. The indifference point is the intersection of cheap and expensive. State the curve convention used for range endpoints. Multiple or absent crossings warrant an interval or an inconclusive result. These curves measure perceptions. They cannot establish demand or profit. [Van Westendorp methodology](https://conjointly.com/products/van-westendorp/)

When there are few buyers, ask about their last comparable purchase, budget owner, alternative and actual trade-offs. Test a concrete offer on comparable new opportunities. Log package, price, reason for loss, decision date and whether the buyer actually signed. Unanswered proposals remain open until the agreed decision window closes.

## Reading a threshold table

In the original range convention, marginal cheapness is where the too-cheap curve meets the complement of the cheap curve. Marginal expensiveness is where the too-expensive curve meets the complement of the expensive curve. Some implementations use different range intersections. Name the convention alongside the numbers so another analyst can reproduce the range. [Method options in pricesensitivitymeter](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/pricesensitivitymeter/pricesensitivitymeter.pdf)

For a quote test, keep one row per eligible opportunity with segment, assignment date, source, package, quote, signed amount, variable cost and decision date. Calculate first-period contribution per eligible prospect as (collected revenue minus associated variable costs) divided by all eligible prospects assigned to that offer. If some opportunities have had only half the usual sales cycle, compare mature groups or revisit later.
