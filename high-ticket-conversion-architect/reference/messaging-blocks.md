# From offer facts to a seven-block page

For each block in the wireframe identify the buyer question, copy and evidence. The opening should let the intended reader recognize their situation. The mechanism should show a real sequence of work and its handoff. The proof block should let buyers inspect an artifact or a result that supports a particular claim.

For a fixed-scope assessment, verified inputs might include a kickoff interview, a review of one workflow and a prioritized handoff. Those facts support a short scope paragraph and a sample agenda. They do not establish a payback period. If the buyer must provide a technical owner, use that condition in the fit section and explain why the owner's availability matters.

Match the CTA to what is actually available. A promised sample requires a shareable sample. A scoping call should identify what information the buyer needs and what decision follows. Verify pricing basis, exclusions and responsibility for implementation before drafting terms.

## Proof copy with limited permissions

For a migration assessment that includes a reconciliation worksheet and a risk register, the proof block can read:

> Your team receives a reconciliation worksheet for comparing the source and destination records, plus a register of migration risks to review before cutover.

Keep the deliverables in the statement of work as the editorial source. If a redacted example is approved for public use, link it alongside the copy. If the method is confirmed but client results are confidential, the block can still describe exactly what the buyer receives.
