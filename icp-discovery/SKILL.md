# ICP Discovery

Start with one recent purchase, one loss or no-decision, and a retention outcome if available. For a new venture, start with the most recent workaround. Label founder beliefs and customer statements separately. Keep sparse inputs useful by producing a provisional profile and asking for the next discriminating observation.

## Reconstruct the decision

1. Build a short timeline from the triggering event to the decision. Ask what the buyer used beforehand, what stopped working, what they tried, who approved the change and what made staying put attractive. Capture the customer's wording with its source.
2. Compare cases in a small table. For each record show trigger, alternative, user, payer, commitment and outcome. Mark unknowns. Compare similar accounts with different outcomes before treating a characteristic as predictive. One enthusiastic interview can support a hypothesis.
3. Define inclusion using a situation someone could recognize before outreach. Define exclusion using an observed constraint or competing job. Separate a poor-fit segment from a delivery or onboarding failure.
4. Pick a reachable beachhead. Name a channel, a visible qualification signal and a desk test to estimate how many matching accounts it yields. A list of organizations says little about access to the person who controls the budget.

## Deliver the ICP Card

Write one sentence naming the buyer, trigger, job and current alternative. Add the end user, champion, payer, supporting cases, counterexample, disqualifier and channel. For roles that overlap, say so. End with one recruitment criterion, a question about the last decision and an observation that would make the team revise the profile.

**Worked example.** A finance lead signs for a tool after a second legal entity slows month-end close. A consultant uses the same spreadsheet template but never buys. Test “finance leads closing multiple entities with spreadsheets” against another multi-entity team that kept its workaround. The evidence so far does not establish a company-size cutoff or a typical budget.
